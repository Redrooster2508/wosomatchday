## 46. "font not nice" (build 26)
- The site used three fonts: Big Shoulders Display (very tall and narrow) for headings and tabs, Archivo for text, and IBM Plex Mono with wide spacing for labels, times, odds and table headers, which looked like a typewriter.
- Tried two sets on the same pages: Archivo throughout (its width axis gives a condensed heading cut) and the Barlow family. Chose Archivo: Barlow's semi-condensed labels were harder to read at 11–12 px. It's now one font request: `Archivo:wdth,wght@62..125,400..900`.
- In `styles.css`, headings set `font-stretch:72%` and other text `font-stretch:normal`, so a heading's width isn't inherited by text inside it. Label and number styles use tabular figures, and their letter spacing is cut to .05em. Small capital labels get `word-spacing:.12em`, because Archivo's narrow space made "CHAMPIONS LEAGUE" read as one word.
- Found while testing: on phones the home page was 432 px wide. The page grid sized its column to the full width of the tab row, which is wider in Archivo than in the narrow heading font. `.wrap` now has `grid-template-columns:minmax(0,1fr)`, so the tabs scroll inside the screen.
- Tested at 390 and 1280 px: home page, Arsenal (phone), Chelsea women (desktop, dark) and Saka (phone). No sideways scroll or page errors.

## 48. "why does it not show player name" (build 27, 2026-11-17)
- The match centre's touch-map picker showed "Player 158678 (35)": Caitlin Foord, in London City v Arsenal on 15 November. ESPN's line-up for the match does include her.
- Cause, in `heat.js` `matchPanel`: the app draws the match centre once before the match summary (which holds the line-ups) arrives, then again after. The first call starts loading the play-by-play; its `draw` kept that call's empty name list, and the second call, made while the play-by-play was still loading, changed nothing. So every player was nameless until the next refresh, which doesn't come for a finished match. The second call also briefly drew the "no pitch positions" message, because it drew with no plays yet.
- Fix: the panel keeps the newest call's context (`mstate.ctx`), and `draw` reads names and the match from it; while the play-by-play is loading, nothing is drawn over "Loading play-by-play…".
- Tested by holding back ESPN's play-by-play for 4 s and calling the panel without and then with line-ups. Before: all 31 players "Player <id>". After: all named, "Caitlin Foord (35)". The same fix is in the men's site (its `heat.js` was identical), as its build 4.

## 49. "add a section for each game on where to watch depending on the region" (build 28, 2026-11-19)
- ESPN's scoreboard lists broadcasters with each match, but only for the United States (`geoBroadcasts` with `region: "us"`): Paramount+ for the WSL, CBS and Paramount+ for the NWSL final, Peacock or USA Network for the Premier League. It has nothing for Liga F. There's no free per-match feed for other countries.
- So the panel combines ESPN's per-match US listing with a researched table of rights holders per competition and region (`broadcasters.json`). Sources were the official UEFA and Premier League pages, laliga.com's per-country operator pages, the NWSL's 2026 territory list, and press reports for the WSL and Liga F. Details and gaps are in [[Where to watch]].
- `watch.js` is new and shared by both sites. The panel sits in the match centre under the match.
- Region detection: time zone first. The browser language is used only for UTC or missing zones, because a Tokyo test browser in en-US was otherwise put in the United States.
- Tested in Shanghai, New York, London (phone, dark), Madrid, Tokyo, Hong Kong and Chicago, on the WSL, Liga F, the NWSL, the Women's Champions League and friendlies. A chosen region survives a reload. No page errors or sideways scroll.
- Also on the men's site (build 5), with its own `broadcasters.json` (Premier League, LaLiga, Champions League), tested from London, Shanghai, New York, Madrid and Paris. Each site's file lists only its own competitions.
